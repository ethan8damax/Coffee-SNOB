# Coffee Snob — Curation Standards

*Draft v1. This is the product. Everything else is packaging.*

---

## The Bar

**Would we send a friend visiting from out of town here, and would we be right?**

If there's hesitation, it's not on the list.

We list five to ten shops per city. Not the ten best available. The ten that clear the bar. If a city only has four, we list four.

---

## Non-Negotiables

A shop must meet all four to be considered.

**1. Sourcing you can trace.**
They can tell you the farm, the region, the importer, or the roaster's relationship. Not necessarily on the menu, but the staff knows. A shop that can't answer "where's this from?" isn't specialty, whatever the sign says.

**2. Roast quality.**
Either they roast well themselves or they buy from someone who does. Dark, flat, or burnt as a house style disqualifies regardless of everything else.

**3. Someone behind the bar cares.**
Dialed espresso. Correct temperature. Attention to the shot. This is observable in about ninety seconds. If drinks come out inconsistent across two visits, it's out.

**4. Two visits minimum.**
Nothing gets listed on one visit. Great shops have bad days and mediocre shops have good ones. We need to know which we saw.

---

## What We Weigh

These don't disqualify on their own, but they separate "Worth the detour" from "Catch a flight."

**Consistency.** The strongest signal of a great shop. Same quality on a Tuesday morning and a Saturday afternoon.

**Menu discipline.** A shorter menu executed well beats a long one executed inconsistently. Twelve syrups is usually a tell.

**Space.** Not a requirement, but it's why people go. Seating, light, sound, whether you'd want to stay. A takeaway window can still be exceptional, it just has to be exceptional at what it is.

**Staff knowledge.** Can they recommend based on your preference, not just read the menu back to you.

**Something distinctive.** The thing you'd describe to someone else. A specific pour method, a house blend worth buying, a room nobody else has, a person behind the bar worth meeting.

**Local standing.** What other specialty roasters and baristas in the city say about it. This is the highest-signal input we have and we should use it more than reviews.

---

## Disqualifiers

Immediate no, regardless of anything else.

- Dark or burnt as a house roast style
- Can't or won't answer basic sourcing questions
- Inconsistent execution across visits
- Coffee is clearly secondary to food, retail, or the aesthetic
- Chains and multi-city groups above roughly ten locations
- Paid placement of any kind, ever

---

## Edge Cases

**Roasters with a café attached.** Judge the café. Great roasting doesn't excuse a bad bar.

**Shops inside hotels, stores, or lobbies.** Eligible if the coffee program is real and independently run. Most aren't.

**Multi-location local shops.** Eligible, but rate each location separately. The flagship and the third location are rarely the same experience.

**New shops.** Wait ninety days minimum. Opening quality isn't steady-state quality.

**A great shop with bad service.** Note it in the write-up, dock the rating, still list it if the coffee earns it. We're rating coffee first.

**Legacy shops coasting on reputation.** Rate what's in the cup today. Reputation isn't a rating.

---

## The Five-to-Ten Rule

Per city, at launch:

- **Fewer than 5 qualified shops:** list what qualifies. A short list is a credible list.
- **More than 10 qualified shops:** raise the bar until it's ten. The constraint is the product.
- **Never pad.** A city with three great shops and two mediocre ones has three great shops.

The list should be small enough that a visitor could realistically do the whole thing in a long weekend. That's the design constraint and it's a good one.

---

## Adding and Removing

**To add:** two visits by someone on the team, notes against the non-negotiables, a second opinion if the call is close.

**To remove:** two consecutive disappointing visits, an ownership or head-roaster change that shows in the cup, or a drift toward volume over quality. Removal is quiet. We don't publish takedowns.

**Owner requests:** always welcome, never a shortcut. The visit still happens. Same standard.

---

## The One-Question Test

When a call is genuinely close, ask this:

> *Would someone fly here for this city's list and feel like the list was honest?*

That's the only reputation we have.

## Open Questions

Things to decide before launch:

- Do we publish these standards publicly, or keep them internal? *(Publishing them is a strong trust signal and a differentiator. It also invites argument.)*
- Do we rate decaf, tea, or food at all, or stay strictly coffee?
- How do we handle a city where the best shop is a cart or a window?
- Who has final say when you and Ryan disagree?
