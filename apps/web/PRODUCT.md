# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Urban professionals, 25–45. Coffee is part of how they explore a place, not caffeine delivery — they have a home setup they already love and they're still hunting. Two avatars:

- **Sofia, 25** — product designer, Greenpoint. Moccamaster and Chemex, nerds out on café design.
- **David, 34** — ceramicist, Echo Park. Morning espresso at home, afternoon escape from the studio.

Write to them as peers who happen to know less about *this specific city* than we do.

## Product Purpose

Coffee Snob is a specialty coffee discovery and social app — Letterboxd for coffee shops, backed by real editorial curation instead of crowdsourced ratings. Five to ten shops per city, chosen against written standards. Users log visits, rate on an effort scale, and share finds.

The wedge is travel: people browse locally and become power users in a new city. Launch markets, priority group: NYC, Atlanta, Nashville, London, Tampa. Also mapped, lower priority: Portland, Seattle, SF, Austin. (US first, global eventually — London is the one non-US market in the priority group.)

This `apps/web` surface is the pre-launch site: a weekly editorial letter (one shop, one roaster, the reason it earned the trip) plus city guides, building the audience ahead of the app.

Success at this stage: pre-launch signups, and city-guide/journal content that reads as genuinely curated — not a marketing funnel.

## Brand Personality

**Core attitude:** playful validation of high standards. We give people permission to care about quality. Locked copy: *"It's okay to be a snob."* / *"Baby, you're a snob."*

Bumper-sticker energy — short, confident, a little cheeky. Authoritative without being uptight. We're the welcoming expert, never a gatekeeper.

Voice rules:
- Short sentences, one idea at a time. Opinions stated as facts.
- Our words: curate, curation, rigorous, picky, snob.
- Banned words: elevate, leverage, streamline, "hidden gem," "vibe," "nestled," "it's not X it's Y."
- Dry more often than funny. Trust the reader — they know what a flat white is.
- Never oversell. Adjectives are what you reach for when you don't have the detail yet; specificity does the work instead.

**The rating system is core brand, not just UI:** five levels shown as chevrons (↑ to ↑↑↑↑↑), each an *effort level* ("Stay home" → "Catch a flight"), never a number. "How far would you go for this?" Never introduce numeric scores or decimal averages — the scale is the brand.

## Anti-references

- Generic listicle coffee blogs and round-ups. No affiliate padding, ever. No paid placement.
- Any interface implying a numeric score, star rating, or decimal average.
- Copy that sells instead of orients — the write-up's job is to say what a place is and whether it's your kind of place, not to hype it.
- Design working notes: moodboard direction not locked between heritage/elegant and Italian/contemporary sticker-design; the sticker direction reads as the better fit for this voice. Where a formal-strategy brand deck conflicts with the voice doc, the voice doc wins.

## Design Principles

1. **The scale is the brand.** Effort-based ratings (chevrons, no numbers) are core product identity, not a styling choice — never let a numeric score sneak into any UI.
2. **Curate ruthlessly, never pad.** Five to ten shops per city; a short, honest list beats a long, padded one. The same restraint applies to content density and page layout — don't inflate for the sake of filling space.
3. **Specificity over adjectives.** Every claim should survive "could this describe something else?" — concrete detail beats generic marketing language, in copy and in visual choices alike.
4. **Welcoming expert, never gatekeeper.** Confident and opinionated, but never exclusionary or precious.
5. **Orient, don't sell.** Someone should be able to act on the page in twenty minutes — clarity and usefulness first, persuasion second.
