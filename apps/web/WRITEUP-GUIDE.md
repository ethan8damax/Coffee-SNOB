# Coffee Snob — Shop Write-Up Guide

*How every café description gets written.*

---

## The Job

The list tells someone where to go. The write-up tells them why, and whether it's their kind of place.

Write for someone standing on an unfamiliar corner with twenty minutes. They've already decided to trust us. Don't sell, orient.

---

## Structure

Every write-up has three moves. They can blur together, but all three should be there.

**1. The thing that makes it what it is.**
One specific, concrete detail. Not "great atmosphere." The corner it sits on, the one drink they're known for, the fact that there's no wifi, the roaster they pull from.

**2. What you'd order.**
Say it directly. This is the single most useful sentence we write and most guides skip it.

**3. Who it's for, or when to go.**
Morning versus afternoon. Solo versus with someone. Laptop-friendly or hostile to laptops. Skip this if it's not distinctive.

---

## Length

**60 to 90 words.** Hard ceiling at 110.

If it takes more than 90 words, the shop probably isn't as distinctive as you think, or you haven't found the detail yet.

---

## Always Mention

- What to order
- The roaster, if they don't roast in-house
- Anything that would surprise or annoy someone who showed up unprepared (cash only, no seating, closes at 1pm, line out the door on weekends)

## Never Mention

- Prices. They change, and it reads cheap.
- Tasting notes copied off the bag.
- The word "hidden gem." The word "vibe." The word "nestled."
- Anything you didn't observe yourself.
- The owner's backstory unless it's actually in the cup.
- Comparisons to chains. We don't punch down.

---

## Voice Reminders

Short sentences. Opinions stated as facts. Dry more often than funny.

Trust the reader. They know what a flat white is.

The write-up should sound like one person who's been there, not a committee. First person plural is fine. Second person is better.

Never oversell a five. If a shop is "Catch a flight," the details will carry it. Adjectives are what you reach for when you don't have the details.

---

## Examples

### Catch a flight

**Hoxton Berlin — Mitte**

A converted print shop with one espresso machine and no menu board. You tell them how you drink coffee and they decide. It sounds insufferable and it works. The house Ethiopian is the reason to come, pulled short and served without ceremony. Twelve seats, all facing the bar, so plan on a conversation. Closed Mondays and they mean it.

*(88 words. The specificity does all the work. No adjective is doing heavy lifting.)*

---

### Make the trip

**Canyon Coffee — Echo Park**

The one everyone in LA names when you ask, and for once the consensus is right. They roast light and pull it clean, which is a harder combination than it sounds. Order the cortado. Small patio, better in the morning before the studio crowd shows up. Bring cash if you want a bag of beans, the card reader is unreliable.

*(70 words. Practical detail earns trust.)*

---

### Worth the detour

**Homecoming — Greenpoint**

Half flower shop, half café, and the coffee holds up better than that arrangement suggests. Sey beans, dialed properly, in a room that's genuinely nice to sit in. Filter over espresso here. Gets crowded by ten on weekends and the seating is not built for laptops, which is either the point or the problem depending on why you came.

*(69 words. Honest about the tradeoff without being negative.)*

---

### If it's on your way

**Devoción — Williamsburg**

The skylight room is the draw and they know it. Coffee is fresh, sourced direct from Colombia, and perfectly fine. Nothing here will change your mind about anything, but if you're already in the neighborhood and want somewhere pleasant to sit for an hour, it does that well. Order the drip.

*(56 words. Damning with faint praise, without being cruel.)*

---

### Stay home

**Example Coffee Co.**

We generally don't write these up. If a shop doesn't clear the bar, it isn't on the list at all. A "Stay home" rating exists for user reviews, not for our editorial picks.

---

## The Test

Read it back and ask: **could this paragraph describe a different shop?**

If yes, you haven't found the detail yet. Go back.
